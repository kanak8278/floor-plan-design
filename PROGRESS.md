- **`BRIEF.ZONE_UNMET` could never fire.** It reads `ctx.brief["_vastu_findings"]`, which nothing wrote, and `vastu_score` ran AFTER `check_brief` in `validate()`. So a brief that says "pooja in the NE" and gets it in the SW produced only the generic `VASTU.POOJA` warning -- never the brief-level failure the rule's own comment says it exists to make. Vastu now runs first and stashes its findings. Verified firing.
- `direct_access` and `adjacent` are now different requirements. Mapping both onto the door check rejects a correct plan: "pooja adjacent to the living" is satisfied by a shared wall, and a door from a living room into a shrine is not what was asked for. New `BRIEF.NOT_TOUCHING` tests a shared boundary; `adjacent` still tests a door. `visual` and `same_floor` have no test yet and come back as `BRIEF.RELATION_UNCHECKED` at weight 0.1 rather than vanishing -- a requirement that disappears from the report looks exactly like one that passed.
- `scripts/eval_json.py`: Project JSON -> door graph + space-syntax table + bathroom kinds + findings by family. The service returns a flat findings list and nothing of the graph it derived them from, and "living is not the core" is unactionable without knowing which room is.
- **Evaluation depends on the `_fpeval` sidecar and the editor never writes it.** Room categories, opening kinds and the plot polygon live in `project["_fpeval"]`; `grep -rn "_fpeval" vendor/openPlan3D/src/` returns nothing. Measured on the same plan with the sidecar stripped: 8 room categories -> 1 (`"indoor"`, which is not a `roomtypes` key, so `canonical()` returns `"unknown"` and every category-driven rule skips), 18 findings across 7 families -> 7 rule ids, of which only `BYLAW.SITE_UNSPECIFIED` and `VASTU.BRAHMASTHAN` still mean anything. `GEO.NO_FRONT_DOOR` fires because front-door identity is in the sidecar too. Any room a user draws by hand is invisible to the rule engine.
- End-to-end, no LLM in the path: synthetic `DesignSpec` -> `brief_from_spec` -> `spec_to_programme` + `spec_adjacency_pairs` -> solve (OPTIMAL on 40x60) -> `to_project` -> JSON on disk -> `from_project` -> validate. 13 rooms, 4 errors / 21 warnings, and the brief family reports the two things the plan did not honour: no foyer, and the utility with no door to the kitchen. The same programme on 30x40 returns INFEASIBLE with `min_area+min_clear_width (jointly)` on all 400 ranked topologies, which is the right answer for 12 rooms on 1200 sqft.
- A brief with the wrong key names is silent. `check_brief` reads `brief["requirements"]["rooms"]`; a dict with top-level `rooms` and `adjacent` produced zero findings and looked like a pass. That is what `brief_from_spec` is for -- one producer, one shape.

## The relational terms were never switched on

- `COUPLING.md`: audit of all 100 rules against the solver's actual mechanisms. Four coupling states, not two — **10 hard** (a CP-SAT constraint group), **23 by construction** (the pipeline cannot produce the violation, so the rule exists for the editor and for ResPlan import, not for us), **42 soft** (a term in the cross-topology key or the surrogate objective), **18 uncoupled** (checked, and nothing anywhere can act on it), **7 data/disclosure** (not defect statements at all). My prediction that most errors were uncoupled was wrong; the mechanisms are richer than I credited, and `forbidden_adjacency` is applied as a hard edge exclusion at `solver.py:769` rather than collected and dropped.
- **Two of the three paths that solve a plan passed no relational constraints at all, then validated it against rules that assume them.** `score.py` calls `topology.solver_pairs` and hands `required_adjacency` / `forbidden_adjacency` / `soft_adjacency` to `LayoutSpec`; `loop.py:161` and `service/app.py` built one from programme + entrance + time limit and nothing else. So on both live paths the 1e7 required-adjacency term multiplied an empty list and `w_soft_adj` weighted nothing. The measured suite figures (54/100 fully passing, 72/100 with zero rule errors) described a configuration neither the repair loop nor the service ran.
- Cross-check on the 11-room 3BHK: of 14 warnings, **9 were uncoupled rules and a 10th was a relational rule whose constraint was never populated on that path.** Only 2 of 14 were a solver that weighed the trade-off and chose otherwise. The residue on a solved plan is almost exactly the uncoupled set, which is what you would predict — coupled findings get optimised away and uncoupled ones cannot be.
- Fixed: one shared `bridge.resolve_scenario` + `bridge.relational_pairs`, called by `loop.py` and `service/app.py`, merging the scenario's preference matrix with the spec's own adjacency wishes. A forbidden pair now wins over a required one carrying the same rooms — silently solving for something the brief forbids is worse than dropping a default. `/generate` reports which terms the solve carried, because a plan solved with none of them is a different artefact from one solved with all of them.
- **`solver_pairs` handed every -1.0 preference to the solver as a HARD edge exclusion, taste included.** On a 50x80 5BHK villa the living-bedroom rule became five hard exclusions — no bedroom could hang off the living room — and with no foyer, stair or store in that programme the door assignment had nowhere left to put them, so the solve fell back to topologies with an interior habitable room. `Pref.hard` now marks the three genuine taboos (WC into kitchen, shrine, dining); every other prohibition is priced at full weight instead, and for *every* pair rather than one representative, because the point of "no bedroom door in the formal living" is that it holds for all five bedrooms. Villa hard exclusions 17 -> 12.
- `bedroom`-`bedroom` emitted `('bed1', 'bed1', -0.5)`. `solver_pairs` took `ha[0]` and `hb[0]` from the same list, so the one preference meant to keep bedrooms from opening into each other weighted a self-pair and did nothing.
- **The A/B says the wiring is a lateral move that exposed a scoring defect.** Four plots (30x40 3BHK, 40x60 4BHK, 25x40 2BHK, 50x80 5BHK), relational terms off then on: relational findings 53 -> 48, warnings 63 -> 62, **errors flat at 9 — and the composition got worse in law terms.** The ON run cleared 2 `DESIGN.DINING_ABUTS_WC`, 2 `DESIGN.WC_VISIBLE_FROM_DINING`, 2 `DESIGN.KITCHEN_SINGLE_ACCESS` and 1 `DESIGN.BEDROOM_THROUGH_TRAFFIC`, and gained 2 `NBC.VENTILATION_HABITABLE` and 1 `DESIGN.NO_WINDOW`. That is paying law to buy design, which the 2e7 > 1e7 priority order exists to prevent.
- The residual cause is the *legitimate* exclusions, not the taste ones: 12 bathroom x dining/kitchen/pooja pairs on a 4-bath villa leave the door assignment too little circulation, and no candidate topology in the 96 tried keeps every habitable room on the perimeter. Arguably the honest reading is that a 5BHK villa programme with no foyer, no stair and no store does not work — but the solver should say so, not ship a windowless bedroom.
- **This is the empirical argument for the lexicographic score.** Counting findings says 9 errors -> 9 errors, a wash, so `loop._score` would accept the swap. A lexicographic vector says the plan moved a defect from level 5 (is it a good house) up to level 2 (is it legal to build), which is strictly worse. Until the score can see that, no change to the solver can be evaluated. Doing that next.
- Tests: 247 pass idle. `test_latency_budget_on_a_ten_room_plan` and the latency half of `test_false_positive_rate_on_real_plans` fail under concurrent load (25 ms against a 10 ms budget) exactly as the README records; every per-rule false-positive budget passed.

## The front door never moves

- **The front door is on the north wall of 89 of 89 suite plans.** Found by the all-89 geometric review, not by the coupling audit, which read the solver's terms and therefore could not see it. `solver.py:1073` places the door only `if ey0 == Y0` — hard-coded to the top edge of the tiling. `road_facing` rotates the ENVELOPE; the door does not move with it. So the entrance position is not a decision variable, `VASTU.ENTRANCE` ("main entrance N/E/NE; SW is the strongest taboo") can never be satisfied or optimised, and the `vastu-*` suite cases cannot be testing what their names imply. When the entrance room does not touch `Y0`, no front door is placed at all and `GEO.NO_FRONT_DOOR` fires.
- `COUPLING.md` corrected: `VASTU.ENTRANCE` moves from soft to uncoupled, 18 -> 19. The lesson is method, not arithmetic — auditing the solver's terms found 18 uncoupled rules; auditing its OUTPUTS found a 19th that the term-level pass could not, because the term exists and the variable it multiplies is constant. Both passes are needed.
- `FLOORPLAN_ISSUES.md` (all 89 plans: visual pass on 35, geometric pass on 89 from the SVG room polygons) supersedes `REVIEW.md` (n=4, visual only). It confirms the pilot's five new `PROG.*` rules at scale and finds far more: master with no attached bath **79/89**, foyer not at the entrance **53/89**, kitchen-bedroom door **22/89**, gate on the opposite boundary from the front door **13/89**, a room reachable only through a bathroom 21 plans, a bathroom whose only door is into another bathroom 13 plans, no toilet reachable without entering a bedroom 28 plans, over 20% of carpet in circulation 10 plans (worst 27%). Two rules confirmed as doing their job: `DESIGN.ENTRANCE_INTO_PRIVATE` and `NBC.WC_OPENS_INTO_KITCHEN` fire zero times in 89 plans.
- `REVIEW.md`'s only durable contribution is the tagging sheet — CAUGHT / SILENT (a rule exists, its own stated intent covers the case, it does not fire) / NO RULE. The SILENT category earned its place immediately: `typology._COMMON` says a shrine "must not share a **wall** or door with a toilet" and `check_typology`'s `separate` branch tests `joined()`, i.e. doors only.

## Still not built

The measurement. `loop._score` returns `(n_err, n_warn, -vastu)` — lexicographic already, but component 1 lumps every family into one count, so a plan that trades two `DESIGN` errors for two `NBC` ventilation errors scores as a tie and wins on the warning tiebreak. The same defect sits in the solver's cross-topology key, which is a weighted SUM dressed as a priority order: at 2e7 / 1e7, **two unmet required adjacencies cost exactly what one windowless habitable room costs**. Until both compare tuples instead of summing big numbers, none of this session's five fixes can be shown to be improvements.
